# The Fraud of Islamic Mint Nusantara

Not too long ago I wrote an academic piece 1 refuting the research of Dr. Ahamed Kameel Meera, where he argued that the authentic standard of the Islamic Dinar was 4.5g of pure (24k) gold. This was important given the fact that certain mints — who are opposed to the genuine work of Sh. Abdalqadir as-Sufi, Sh. Umar Vadillo and World Islamic Mint — were taking advantage of this research as their primary academic resource.
Needless to say, the refutation caused some problems for these minters, especially Islamic Mint Nusantara, which promotes what they call the “Open Mithqal Standard”; essentially a way of determining the weight and purity of the dinar entirely relative to the local populations that wish to use the currency:
It should be noted that OMS is a Standard and we in no way are asserting that it is THE standard, i.e there are other standards as well who have merit. Mints should choose the standard which best meets the requirements of their people.2
How something so relative can be considered a “standard” valid with “other standards” is a remarkable disregard for logic, but to be fair,we’ll be taking a closer look at their argument shortly.
A little while after my refutation, those supporters from Islamic Mint Nusantara decided to construct a rebuttal. Rather than return fire with an appropriate academic response, what we got instead was a copy and paste diatribe of a Facebook note with additional texts added from my own work3. No academic references were mentioned therein. Before I began to write this piece I first assessed whether or not addressing this note would be a productive use of my time. I eventually determined that, in the very least, I could further elucidate some historical references that would put this issue to rest once and for all and spare us any more desperate attempts at justifications from these opposing minters. In doing so, we might finally find some peace and be able to focus on more important things. So, as per my duty to this ummah and the work of Allah, I’ll make this quick and to the point.
IMN relies on some major fallacies to back their position:
That the Dinar is based on the Solidus
That Arab weights and measures were similar to the Romans
That the historical reality of varying weights and measures in the Islamic world constitutes as a prescriptive fact of an “open standard”
Note that (1-2) have already been dealt with in full in my previous article4. What this article wishes to address is number (3). Here, IMN relies primarily on Ibn Khaldun for support, so we shall use his works to refute their own position. They first suggest:
Mithqal as unit of weight determined by grains of Barley or حَبَّةً شَعِيرَةً that had been known in Arab and Rome. Islamic jurisprudence stated that 1 Mithqal is 72 grains of Barley cut in the both edges (Ibn Khaldun, Al-Muqaddimah). So it is a fallacy to determine “mithqal” based on archeological objects.5
The first mistake here that one should note is the claim that “it is a fallacy to determine ‘mithqal’ based on archeological objects”. In other words, what IMN is referring to here is that we cannot rely on the actual coins themselves from the past, nor their glass weights, to determine the appropriate weights. The second mistake is that they assume that Ibn Khaldun here is justifying a weight of the mithqal (which determines the dinar weight), based on the application of a general weight of barley grains with no concern for their place of origin. Both assumptions can be refuted quite easily by simply referencing one authentic hadith and Ibn Khaldun himself.
The hadith is from Abu Dawud, the same that was used by Dr. Kameel in his research, supported by IMN:
“The Prophet (pbuh) said: (The standard) weight is the weight of the people of Mecca, and the (standard) measure is the measure of the people of Medina”6
In other words, the standard weights and measure are those from the people of Mecca and Medina — not from the people of Rome, Kufa (Iraq) or Timbuktu. Ibn Marwan was the one who manifested this theoretical knowledge and made sure that the standard was put into place, as explained in my previous article and also the work of Ibn Khaldun several centuries before. In a statement from his famous Muqaddimah, Ibn Khaldun makes is very clear that the religious-legal standard of the Dinar was firmly enforced by Ibn Marwan:
When ‘Abd-al-Malik saw fit to use the mint to protect against fraud the two coins (the gold dinar and the silver dirham) that were current in Muslim transactions, he determined their values as what they had been in the time of ‘Umar….Conditions called for individual specification of the value and the weight of dirhams and dinars,in accordance with the religious law, in order to obviate the (constant) obligation to determine their value. This (situation) developed in the days of ‘Abd-al-Malik. He specified the individual value of (the dinar and the dirham) outside (in real money), as it had been in theory.7
Note what is clearly being stated here: Ibn Marwan was following the religious obligations of Islam by specifying the actual value of the Dinar and Dirham as practiced by the early sahaba. Not only that, but he did so in order that the values no longer were burdensome to determine. The value that was guarded was 4.25 grams, based on the weights and measures of Mecca and Medina.
Ibn Khaldun goes on to assert that later dynasties went against this standard:
Later on, officials of the mint in the various dynasties deliberately disregarded the legal value of dinar and dirham. Their value became different in the different regions. The people reverted to a theoretical knowledge of (the legal dinar and dirham), as had been the case at the beginning of Islam. The inhabitants of every region calculated the legal tariffs in their own coinage, according to the relationship that they knew existed between the (actual) value of (dirhams and dinars in their coinage) and the legal value.8
Note, he states that people reverted to a “theoretical knowledge” (grain weights) to determine the weights of their dinar and dirham, disregarding the legal value as established by the religion and enforced by Ibn Marwan:
The truth is that the value of (legal dirhams and dinars) was known at the (earliest) times (of Islam) for the implementation of laws involving tariffs based on (legal dirhams and dinars),but their value was not individually specified outside [until Ibn Marwan]. It was an internal custom of the Muslims, which had become accepted under the influence of the religious law,and which used a fixed value and weight for (dirhams and dinars).9
The fact that various standards existed in the past is not an excuse to make that an “open standard” for the dinar and dirham. Per IMN’s own references, they have misled their own supporters and have lied to the world about the legal value of the dinar and dirham. Rather than uphold the actual standards for this very important application of our faith, they have whored it out to the whims and desires of local populations without any regard for what the religion teaches and the authorities in support of it. They are no other than the same as those dynasties that deliberately disregarded what was set in stone before them so as to fulfill their own agendas.
