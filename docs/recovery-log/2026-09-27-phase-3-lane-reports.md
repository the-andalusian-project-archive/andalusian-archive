the title and his argument. A paper he co-wrote is B. Never decide by the word *interview*, *dialogue* or *debate* in a
title. A thing counted in two buckets is a bug: every countable item has exactly one category letter and `counted: true`.

## 5. Transcript rendering — the three cases, verified on the built site

`_layouts/video.html` decides from the data, never from guesswork. Checked on all 68 rendered pages: **56 / 12 cases
correct, disclaimer byte-exact on all 56, 10 alternates rendered and attributed, 0 failures.**

**Case 1 — a transcript exists (56 pages).** The disclaimer is printed **verbatim**:

> *Transcript by machine — accuracy isn't perfect, so it shouldn't be used in polemics or debate material as an authoritative source.*

followed by the capture's real provenance from `transcript_coverage.json` — file name, line count, language, and how it
was produced (`whisper-base-local`, `whisper-tiny-local`, `youtube-auto-captions`) — and, where the archive prints the
transcript text on an article page, a link to it. That link's join is the video's YouTube watch URL inside the article's
transcript heading, which is the only place the builder embeds it, so a page that merely *links* to the video is not
mistaken for one that prints its text. Where no article prints the text, the page says so plainly: *"The capture is
preserved in the archive; its text is not printed on this page."*

**Case 2 — `transcripts: 0` (12 pages).** One neutral line: *"Transcript not yet available for this entry."* It does not
promise a transcript, does not give a date, and links no file.

**Case 3 — re-parented `alternates` (10 across 5 pages).** Each is named, attributed and described as an **additional
transcript of the same recording, not a separate recording**: title, producer, language, line count, capture file,
video id, and the date it was re-parented. They are printed, not summarised away — e.g. `NyAVl7RsEOs` shows both
`2tsI80MDUOI` and `EK5oppX6C2U` in full.

**The one place I went past the literal brief, and why.** `G47Stp3pLss` is a `transcripts: 0` entry that *does* have a
preserved 15,048-line capture, deliberately unattached by user decision. A bare "not yet available" would have hidden a
transcript the archive holds, against the brief's own "never delete or hide a transcript". So that page carries the
neutral line **and** a factual line naming the preserved capture, its size and why it is not attached. No promise, no
dead link, nothing hidden.

## 8. Two bugs found and fixed along the way

### 8.1 The site had no homepage and no Blog Posts index (pre-existing, high severity)

`index.md` and `articles.md` both began with a **UTF-8 BOM**, so Jekyll did not recognise their front matter and
treated them as static files. The build "succeeded" and copied them verbatim: `_site/index.md` and `_site/articles.md`
existed as raw markdown, and `_site/index.html` / `_site/articles/index.html` did not exist. Every other root page
(`papers.md`, `videos.md`, `search.md`, `timeline.md`) is BOM-free and built correctly, which is why nothing noticed.

The homepage is the subject of Task 2, so this had to be fixed: **BOM stripped from both files**; the homepage and the
Blog Posts index now build. A repo-wide scan confirms no other BOM in anything Jekyll renders.

I could not establish from the SDD workspace whether the homepage was ever live — no report mentions it. Worth the
controller checking the deployed site, because if the BOM predates this task then the archive has been published
without a homepage.

*(Side effect: stripping the BOM rewrote `index.md` with LF endings, so its diff is whole-file rather than the block I
added. Content is otherwise unchanged.)*

### 8.2 Nine of twenty paper pages claimed a publication date the archive does not have (pre-existing)

`paper.html` ran `page.publication_date` through Liquid's `date` filter. Liquid reads an **all-digits string as a Unix
timestamp**, so the year-only value `"2020"` was rendered as **"January 1970"**. Fixed by printing the raw source string,
which is the only value the page can trust: `build_collections.py` deliberately keeps it in `publication_date` and emits
a Jekyll `date` only for full YYYY-MM-DD values, precisely because year-only and year-month values are fatal to
Jekyll's Date. All 10 populated dates now render the true source value; the 9 undated papers render no date at all.

### 1.5 Getting the words out of the captures

`parse_capture(path)` returns `[(start, end, text)]`.

- **WebVTT** — drops the `WEBVTT` header, `Kind:` / `Language:` / `NOTE` lines, bare cue numbers, the `-->` timing lines, and the inline `<00:00:03.840><c>` timing / `<v Name>` voice tags. The caption text itself is untouched.
- **whisper JSON** — reads `segments` as-is.
- **the rolling window.** YouTube's auto-captions write every caption line twice: once as a long cue that repeats the previous line plus the new words, and once as a ~10 ms "settled" cue holding only the new words. Keeping both prints each line up to three times — which is exactly what `transcribe.load_segments()` does, and therefore what the 15 transcripts already embedded in `_articles/` look like (see §9.1). I verified the format on all 29 `.vtt` files: settled cues are 48–50 % of all cues in every one, and settled words are ~1/3 of all words, consistently. The rule is: keep the settled cue of each line (duration ≤ 50 ms), drop the rolling echo; a capture in any other caption format has no settled cues and falls back to the plain parse rather than losing its text. Consecutive identical lines are dropped (auto-caption echo, not speech). **The words are unchanged; each is printed once.**

`capture_paragraphs(cues)` starts a new paragraph when consecutive cue *starts* are more than 2.0 s apart — the same rule `transcribe.vtt_to_article_md()` already uses, so a published transcript's paragraph shape matches the transcript blocks already on the site.

## 7. Design decisions worth a second opinion

1. **A document per capture, not per video.** 68 pages rather than 58. The alternative — one page per recording with several transcripts inside it — cannot express "this transcript belongs to upload X, which left the catalogue", and it would bury 10 re-parented captures in a page that looks like one transcript. The brief asked for attribution, so the unit is the capture.
2. **The rolling-window echo is dropped.** The brief asked for clean paragraphs and for the words not to be altered. Those two are compatible here because the echo is the *same words* written twice by the caption file, and the settled cue is the caption's own final rendering of the line. Every transcript page states the rule. This does mean the transcript pages and the 3 VTT-based transcripts in `_articles/` render the same recording differently (§9.1).
3. **`G47Stp3pLss` published but not linked from its video page** (§1.9). The other reading — link it from the video page under the neutral note — is defensible; I took the one that keeps the user's "deliberately not attached" decision and the brief's "no implication that one is coming" both intact.
4. **A `Transcripts` nav entry.** Eight items in the primary nav. It is the only way a reader reaches a transcript that is not attached to a video page, and the transcripts are now a first-class part of the archive.
5. **`kramdown` escaping instead of a config change.** Turning `smart_quotes` off site-wide would have altered the rendering of every existing page. Escaping `'` in the source is local and provably renders U+0027.

## R2. Residue 2 — the tripled caption echo, and one file I did not touch

### R2.1 How the residue was found, and what it actually is

`_data/transcript_coverage.json` + `TRANSCRIPT_MAP` give the exact set: of the 15 article transcripts, **3 are backed by a YouTube auto-caption `.vtt`** and 12 by a local whisper JSON. The `.vtt` three are the echoed ones, because `transcribe.load_segments()` keeps YouTube's rolling caption window (every caption line written twice: once prefixed with the previous line, once on its own), so each line printed up to three times. The whisper files have no window and no echo.

**The fix is one line, in one place.** `transcript_section()` read the cues with `load_segments(row)`; it now reads them with `parse_capture()`, the same reader the published transcript pages use. I did **not** change `transcribe.py`, so `load_segments()` remains available and unchanged for anything else that wants it.

That is why only 3 of the 15 article files changed and the other 12 are byte-identical — verified by `git status _articles/`, which lists exactly three files. I checked whether the whisper path *could* have shifted (42 byte-identical adjacent whisper segments exist corpus-wide, 15 of them inside the mapped videos) and confirmed it does not; had it, I would have had to decide whether a repeated "Okay." is an echo or speech, and it does not arise.
