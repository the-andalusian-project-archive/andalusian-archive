<h1>Foundational Questions of Belief</h1>

<p class="lead">Okay, but how would I even know God is real — and isn't it a bit much to say faith is just built into me?</p>

<p>The foundational layer only: what kind of thing God is claimed to be (not a 'thing' at all), how a finite being could come to know the Absolute, and the four classical routes by which God's existence is held to be provable.</p>

<p class="note">The archive records this work. It does not endorse it, and it is not
a religious authority.</p>

<h3 class="question">what are the four ways god existence is proven in islam</h3>

> Here, Allah states that the signs which point to Truth can be found “in the horizons” (external) “and within us” (internal). As such, the principle arguments for God’s existence may also be divided under these two categories. This dichotomy results in various methods by which such arguments may be validated. For instance, Shaykh Abu ‘Abd Allah Muhammad ibn Saalih ibn Muhammad ibn al-Uthaymeen at-Tamimi categorizes these methods into four in his Commentary on the Three Fundamental Principles of Islam:
His existence, Exalted is He, may be proven by the Fiṭrah (one’s natural inclination), the intellect [Aql], the legislation [Shari’ah], and the senses [Hawass].[4]

<h2>What to know before you rely on this page</h2>
<ul class="caution-list">
<li>**This is the smallest cluster on the site and it covers only half of what its label implies.** The corpus holds no creed text and no exposition of the pillars of faith; searching for tawhid, aqeedah, the shahada and the fundamentals across every recovered work and transcript returns essentially nothing.</li><li>**There is no worked proof of God's existence anywhere here.** He explicitly declines both the Kalam and the contingency arguments. A reader searching this page for a classical proof will not find one, and should not infer that the archive holds one.</li><li>What the cluster does contain is the "No-thing" pair of essays, which argue that absolute non-existence is incoherent and that a "thing" is the only candidate for the Absolute. They are short, and they are about language as much as about metaphysics.</li><li>*Adam Is No Myth* is a republication of Dr David Jalajel's response to his own paper, and is not the subject's argument. It is listed below and not quoted here.</li>
</ul>
