<h2>What the archive should be careful about</h2>
<div class="prose-note">
<p>READ THE SOURCE, NOT THE TITLE - several seed titles do not behave the way the title suggests, and the archive should be careful here. (1) 'The 3 Isms of Atheism' is wayback_only: _articles/the-3-isms-of-atheism.md is a 552-byte stub with a Wayback link and no body text anywhere in this repository, so nothing in this cluster is quoted from it. (2) 'Islamic Intuitionism: The Case Against Atheistic Evidentialism' (the MA thesis) is deliberately NOT mirrored - _papers/islamic-intuitionism-the-case-against-atheistic-evidentialism.md records that the 24-page repository excerpt was withdrawn from the served tree because it reproduces personal identifiers, and the full text is only at the IIUM Student Repository. (3) 'Deconstructing Contemporary Atheist Thought' is held as a PDF only (_papers/deconstructing-contemporary-atheist-thought.pdf, 31 pp.); the _papers/ markdown is metadata only, so nothing here is quoted from it. Those two papers are the formal, citable versions of the fitrah/evidentialism argument and are the single biggest gap in this cluster - a maintainer must not invent quotes for them. THIN STUBS. 'Understanding Atheism', 'Atheism: Doubting Your Doubts', 'Hard Questions: Answering Doubts About Islam' and 'The Atheistic Worldview vs The Qur'anic Worldview' are video posts whose _posts/ files are embed-stub pages (109-178 words, plus scraped ad-script fragments) and are flagged needs_review: true. Each carries exactly one quotable sentence (a caption); the real arguments live in the _transcripts/ files, which is where the quote load for these titles actually sits. 'The Atheistic Worldview vs The Qur'anic Worldview' is the thinnest of the four - its only quotable line is 'A discussion I had on Reviving Faith Radio (August 27, 2017).' and it is marked needs_review: true. Do not build a reader-facing claim on it. WHAT IS ACTUALLY STRONG HERE. The load-bearing material is (a) the two-part essay 'The Rationality of Believing in God Without Evidence', which is the only place the full written argument lives, and (b) the machine transcripts of the lectures. 'Understanding Atheism' sessions 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 carry the evidentialism/scientism/burden-of-proof material, and Session 1 is the cleanest taxonomy of the field (weak vs strong atheism, evidentialism, scientism, humanism, progressionism, anti-theism, plus six named atheist arguments). Session 2 is where his own reply-argument to George Smith's naturalism is laid out in full. Session 4 is not a philosophy session - it is 'common objections against Islam' and the Aisha's-age case study - but it does carry the suppressed-evidence/cherry-picking worked example, so it is here on that basis only. MACHINE TRANSCRIPT WARNING. Every _transcripts/ file carries its own front-matter disclaimer: 'Transcript by machine - accuracy isn't perfect, so it shouldn't be used in polemics or debate material as an authoritative source.' The Whisper output is visibly error-prone and is reproduced exactly as generated: apostrophes appear as a literal backslash apostrophe, proper nouns are mangled (Pitra/fitra for fiṭrah, Fetro, Yuthafro for Euthyphro, Victor Stanger for Stenger, Thomas Koon for Kuhn, Ieqalema/garbled phrases for ulama), and the punctuation is unreliable. These quotes are safe to cite as evidence of what was said on the recording and unsafe to quote as polished argument; any reader-facing page should say so. Duplicate uploads exist as separate files (e.g. transcript-2tsI80MDUOI-duplicate-upload.md and transcript-EK5oppX6C2U-duplicate-upload.md are both Session 1) - cite the canonical capture, not the duplicates. CONTRADICTS / COMPLICATES THE CLUSTER LABEL. Three things. (1) The label says 'doubt and evidence', but the two essay parts argue that belief in God is NOT a doxastic judgement assessed against evidence at all - the fitrah is described as a set process (Reception, Impression, Configuration, Projection) that 'can never invalidate itself', and evidence is 'a response to data that challenges what we think we know; it is not the way we know'. That is a stronger claim than 'belief can be reasonable without proof', and it puts the material at odds with a lot of ordinary philosophy-of-religion framing, including the evidentialism it is written against. A topic page built on this cluster should not be summarised as a balanced 'faith vs evidence' debate. (2) The best-known text explicitly disclaims being an attack on science: 'I am in no way attacking science in this essay', and he criticises atheists for not knowing what science is. A careless headline ('belief beats science') would misstate the source. (3) 'Rational doubt' is doing two different jobs in this material. In the essays, doubt is a trigger for evidence. In the pragmatic lecture 'Atheism: Doubting Your Doubts' doubt is diagnosed as psychological and social, and the stated conclusion is that rational argument is usually not what moves anyone: 'I am not an individual who actually believes that you can convince most people through logical arguments.' A reader looking for 'how do I argue someone out of doubt' will not find that here, and a cluster page that implies otherwise will mislead. SCOPE. The 'A Muslim's Guide to Science and Scientism' lectures (5 parts) are included only where the transcript is actually about the epistemology of belief and the atheist demand for evidence (Parts 1 and 3). Parts 2, 4 and 5 and the rest of the series belong primarily to a science-and-methods cluster, and parts of them are about Islamic history of science rather than doubt. Likewise 'Nothing' is a genuine border case: it is a short metaphysics note arguing that absolute non-existence is incoherent, which touches atheism through the nothingness question rather than through evidence, and it is included with one quote and no more. The ex-Muslim and apostasy material ('Ex-Muslims, Pragmatism, and Islamic Futurism', '4 Lies Muslims Tell About Ex-Muslims', 'Muslim vs. Ex-Muslim', 'Apostasy Beyond the Rhetoric') was surveyed and deliberately excluded: it is about leaving the faith, not about atheism, doubt-as-argument, or evidence, and would sit better in a separate ex-belief cluster.
