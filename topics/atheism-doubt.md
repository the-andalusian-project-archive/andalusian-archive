<h1>Atheism, Doubt and Evidence</h1>

<p class="lead">I believe in God but I can't prove it with science - how is that still rational, and what do I actually say when an atheist demands evidence?</p>

<p>How Al-Andalusi answers the atheist demand for evidence: belief without proof, weak versus strong atheism, the fitrah as a non-evidential route to knowledge, and the argument that requiring evidence for everything is itself unevidenced.</p>

<p class="note">The archive records this work. It does not endorse it, and it is not
a religious authority.</p>

<h2>What to know before you rely on this page</h2>
<ul class="caution-list">
<li>This is not a 'faith versus evidence' debate. The essays argue that belief in God is not the kind of thing a demand for evidence can be levelled at, and that treating it that way misdescribes what is being claimed.</li><li>He explicitly says he is not attacking science: "I am in no way attacking science in this essay." A headline claiming these works pit belief against science would misstate them.</li><li>"Doubt" does two different jobs here. In the essays it is what makes you look for evidence. In *Doubting Your Doubts* it is diagnosed as psychological, and the stated position is that most people cannot be argued out of doubt by logic. A reader looking for a technique for arguing someone out of doubt will not find one here.</li><li>Three of the works people expect on this page are not readable in it. *The 3 Isms of Atheism* survives only as a Wayback listing; *Islamic Intuitionism* and *Deconstructing Contemporary Atheist Thought* are held as metadata and a PDF respectively.</li>
</ul>
