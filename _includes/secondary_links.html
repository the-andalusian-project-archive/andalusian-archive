{%- comment -%} Third-party back-references, 2026-09-28. `/sources/` gives a reader the list of other people's writing about this author. This is the other direction: when the record clearly concerns one item the archive holds, the item itself says so, and says what the relationship is. That "what" matters more than the link. A reprint, a critique and a biography are not the same kind of evidence, and a reader who sees only "related" cannot tell whether they are looking at a copy of the text, an argument against it, or somebody's description of the author. So each entry carries its own `how` string, which is written by scripts/relate_secondary_sources.py and states the basis of the match: a title match, a listing that names it, or a third-party page that carries the item. Lookup is by `page.url` against `_data/secondary_links.json`'s `by_url` index, with a percent-decoded fallback for the same reason as topic_links.html: some permalinks carry `%20` from their original WordPress slugs. This block is deliberately not shown on the archive's own about, method and index pages. Those are not items anyone writes a third-party record about, and an empty heading on every one of them would be noise. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign sl = site.data.secondary_links -%} {%- assign hits = nil -%} {%- if sl and sl.by_url and sl.by_url[page.url] -%} {%- assign hits = sl.by_url[page.url] -%} {%- endif -%} {%- unless hits -%} {%- for pair in sl.by_url -%} {%- if pair[0] == page.url -%} {%- assign hits = pair[1] -%} {%- endif -%} {%- endfor -%} {%- endunless -%} {%- if hits and hits.size > 0 -%}
